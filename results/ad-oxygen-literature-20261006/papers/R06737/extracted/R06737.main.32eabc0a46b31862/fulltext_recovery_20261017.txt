Abstract 
21 
Anaerobic digestion (AD) is a promising technology to recover bioenergy from biodegradable 
22 
biomass, including cellulosic wastes. Through a few fractionation/separation techniques, 
23 
cellulose has demonstrated its potential in AD, but the performance of the process is rather 
24 
substrate-specific as cellulolysis bacteria are sensitive to the enzyme-substrate interactions. 
25 
Cellulosome is a self-assembled enzyme complex with many functionalized modules in the 
26 
bacteria, which has been gradually studied, however the genomic fingerprints of the culture-
27 
specific cellulosome in AD are relatively unclear especially under processing conditions. To 
28 
clarify the key factors affecting the cellulosome induced cellulolysis, this review summarized 
29 
the most recent publications of AD regarding the fates of cellulose, sources and functional 
30 
genes of cellulosome, and omics methods for functional analyses. Different processes for 
31 
organic treatment including applying food grinds in sewer, biomass valorization, cellulose 
32 
fractionation, micro-aeration, and enzymatic hydrolysis enhanced fermentation, were 
33 
highlighted to support the sustainable development of AD technology. 
34 
 
35 
Keyword: Anaerobic digestion, cellulolysis, biomass, cellulosome, cellulosome-embedded 
36 
prokaryotes

1. Cellulosic Substrates in AD 
55 
Climate change has started to demonstrate its dramatic impacts on human society and 
56 
global environment. Anaerobic digestion (AD) among the biochemical conversion practices to 
57 
reduce greenhouse gases emissions (e.g., non-point sources CH4) has become a critical trend 
58 
toward decarbonized economy (Meckling et al., 2017). AD is a widely used technology for 
59 
waste management, energy recovery, and biorefinery for various organic wastes and biomass. 
60 
Lignocellulose has significant potential for the production of biogas and biochemicals due to 
61 
its high energy value building block components in the substrate, e.g., cellulose. Cellulose is 
62 
an abundant and valuable resources in food waste (FW); agriculture waste (AW); and primary 
63 
sludge (PS) from the municipal wastewater treatment system (Li et al., 2021; Zhuang et al., 
64 
2020). The predicted global production of FW will reach 2.5 billion tons in fresh weight by 
65 
2025 (Liu et al., 2020), of which its compositions differ significantly among regions and 
66 
sources (e.g., family, catering, and food industry) (Wang et al., 2020a), and carbohydrates 
67 
contributed from cereal, staple food, and lignocellulose account for approximately 35.5-69.0% 
68 
of FW by weight (Zou et al., 2020). AW is the largest source of cellulosic biomass, of which 
69 
its annual production is over 3 billion tons in fresh weight; the global AW is expected to 
70 
increase by 60% higher than the current value by 2050 (Tripathi et al., 2019). Finally, cellulose 
71 
in municipal wastewater accounts for 25-30% of total solids before being treated in the sewage 
72 
treatment works (Ahmed et al., 2019). The cellulosic biomass contributed considerably to the 
73 
total organic loads, and can increase energy consumption (through aeration) of the overall 
74 
system if not separated (as PS) from the influent (Ahmed et al., 2019). 
75 
With the potential to produce easily digestible glucose, cellulose-rich solids have 
76 
demonstrated its value in AD (Crutchik et al., 2018). However, the hydrolysis rate of cellulose 
77 
has often become the rate limiting factor of the AD. The fundamental genomic approach to 
78 
investigate the impacts is also limited. Based on the recent publications, this paper aims to 
79 


[PDF page 7]
5 
provide an overview of the publication within the past five years to expend the technical and 
80 
economic feasibilities of AD. While the substrate-related factors regarding to this approach 
81 
have been reviewed elsewhere (Wang et al., 2019), this study will focus more on the bio-related 
82 
factors such as cellulose and cellulosome characterization; cellulose degrading microbes/genes; 
83 
genomic foundation of cellulose hydrolysis; and genomic driven AD applications toward more 
84 
effective and efficient operation.

2. Hydrolysis of Lignocellulosic Biomass in AD 
87 
2.1 Characteristics lignocellulosic biomass and their hydrolysis in AD 
88 
The average biogas production of different lignocellulosic and cellulosic substrates with 
89 
different chemical compositions, treatment processes, and biogas productivities have been 
90 
demonstrated in Table 1. Although due to the high quantity in the substrate the hydrolysis of 
91 
the cellulosic component has served as the main concern of the AD, the rates of cellulolysis 
92 
can be severely interfered by the other building block components in the plant cell wall micro-
93 
structure. Lignocellulosic biomass is composed of cellulose, hemicellulose, lignin, and small 
94 
amounts of other compounds (such as protein and pectin). Pabón-Pereira et al. (2020; 2012) 
95 
indicated that cellulosic biomass demonstrated quite different methanogenesis potential, i.e., 
96 
0.14 to 0.37 L CH4∙g VS-1 under biochemical methane potential (BMP) tests. The conceptual 
97 
structure of the plant cell wall, with the spatial arrangement and related hydrolyzing enzyme 
98 
for each building block chemicals has been demonstrated in Figure 3 and 4. 
99 
Cellulose is the key component of plant cell walls, which exists in fiber forms embedded 
100 
in a complex matrix of other building block chemicals . The cellulosic long-chain is composed 
101 
of hundreds to thousands ᴅ-glucopyranose units connected with β-1,4-glycosidic linkages . The 
102 
micro-fibers are attached in line through hydrogen bonds, and the various forms and quantities 
103 
of the bonding resulted in the unique crystalline and amorphous domains of the biopolymer 
104 


[PDF page 8]
6 
(Pensupa et al., 2017).  As the arrangement patterns of the cellulose differ significantly from 
105 
the species and parts of the plants, different crystallinity, hydrophilicity, and accessibility of 
106 
enzyme to cellulose (Liu et al., 2016) are formed and resulted in different biodegradabilities in 
107 
the AD. Popovic et al. (2019) reported that the hydrolysis of amorphous cellulose to aqueous 
108 
glucose has negative change in Gibbs free energy (at 0 to 100°C), indicating that the reaction 
109 
is thermodynamically favorable, i.e., the chemical reaction could happen without extra energy 
110 
input. However, the change in Gibbs free energy from crystal cellulose to aqueous glucose 
111 
could vary from -6.6 to 3.0 kJ/mol depending on the types of crystal forms and reaction 
112 
temperature, suggesting increased difficulties in hydrolysis of crystalline cellulose. Although 
113 
the reaction is thermodynamic favorable (Gibbs free energy is negative) the reaction rate was 
114 
still affected by the physical structure of the substrate and the catalyst factor in the system. 
115 
During hydrolysis, the crystal cellulose is peeled off layer by layer, and can be accelerated by 
116 
changing the ultrastructure from cellulose I to cellulose II or cellulose III (Horikawa et al., 
117 
2013; Wada et al., 2010). 
118 
Hemicelluloses are polysaccharides attached amorphously with the cellulosic fibers. Xylan 
119 
(a five carbon, C5 sugar) and mannan (a C6 sugar) are the most abundant hemicellulose in 
120 
hardwood/herbal and softwood, respectively. Different enzyme systems are responsible to the 
121 
digestion of hemicelluloses and cellulose. Previous BMP tests showed that the hydrolysis rate 
122 
of hemicellulose was faster than that of cellulose and pure cellulose showed higher methane 
123 
potential than hemicellulose (Li et al., 2018a). Thermodynamically, hemicellulose hydrolysis 
124 
is an endotherm and hence extra energy or additional product is needed in the reaction (Tizazu 
125 
& Moholkar, 2018). Some hemicellulose could also inhibit cellulolysis (Chen et al., 2020). 
126 
However, pentose and hexose are important carbon and energy sources to be completely 
127 
utilized in an AD (Chen et al., 2019). 
128 


[PDF page 9]
7 
Lignin is a three-dimensional biopolymer composed of three monolignols (Islam et al., 
129 
2020). It fills up the space among the cellulose, hemicellulose, and pectin, and forms into a 
130 
crosslinked network structure with various types of linkages, i.e., covalent, hydrogen bonds, 
131 
and van der Waals forces (Speight, 2020). The massive aromatic nucleus in lignin matrix makes 
132 
it extremely recalcitrant against chemical and biological degradation (Khan & Ahring, 2021). 
133 
The hydrolysis of lignocellulosic structure is related to many substrate-related interactions 
134 
between enzymes and lignin. The biodegradability of lignocellulosic biomass in 
135 
methanogenesis AD could vary from 0.1% to 76.4%, and the lag phase could range from 0 to 
136 
27.4 days (Li et al., 2018a; Ma et al., 2019). Lignin may absorb and/or deactivate the enzymes 
137 
(Li et al., 2018b). Complete decomposition of lignin is a slow process that could last for 
138 
centuries (Zabel & Morrell, 2020). Lignin depolymerization in AD are mainly carried out by 
139 
Spirochaeta and Chitinophaga (Pandit et al., 2016). The biodegradation process starts from 
140 
breakages of side chains, functional groups, and/or aromatic nucleus, and complete degradation 
141 
of lignin (breakage of the aromatic rings) have never been observed anaerobically. With limited 
142 
energy yield lignin depolymerization often couples with the metabolisms of easily degradable 
143 
substrates. Some studies found that sulfate reducing bacteria of Desulfomicrobium, 
144 
Desulfomicrobium, and Desulfobulbus genus could decompose lignin by using sulfate as 
145 
election acceptor (Wang et al., 2013; Yamashita et al., 2011).

2.2 Enzymatic system for cellulolysis and glycoside hydrolase families  
148 
Cellulose degradation in bioconversion process has been mainly carried out by cellulase 
149 
produced from anaerobic or aerobic microorganisms, such as bacteria and fungi. Cellulases 
150 
include a few types of enzymes which can be divided into three categories according to the 
151 
catalytic functions, i.e., exoglucanases (1,4-D-glucan glucanohydrolases and 1,4-Dglucan 
152 
cellobiohydrolases), endoglucanases (1,4-D-glucan-4-glucanohydrolases), and β-glucosidases 
153 


[PDF page 10]
8 
(β-glucoside glucohydrolases) (Bhati & Sharma, 2020; Lynd et al., 2002; Schwarz, 2001). 
154 
Complete hydrolysis of cellulose relies on the synergetic reactions of the enzymes, i.e., 
155 
exoglucanases hydrolyze the crystalline cellulose from either the reducing end or non-reducing 
156 
end of the cellulosic chain (Teeri, 1997; Zhang et al., 2018); endoglucanases randomly break 
157 
the glycosidic linkages in the amorphous  cellulose, releasing large numbers of short-chain 
158 
cellulose and available free ends for exoglucanases (Brunecky et al., 2017); and β-glucosidases 
159 
break the glycosidic linkages of di-glucosides, alkyl/aryl glucosides, cyanogenic glucosides, 
160 
and oligoglucosides for glucose (Thapa et al., 2020). In this process, the main rate-limiting step 
161 
is the hydrolysis of crystalline cellulose (by exoglucanases), while the number of 
162 
exoglucanases in most AD system is too low to support the cellulolysis at high efficiency.  
163 
In addition to cellulases, hemicellulases also participate in lignocellulose hydrolysis. The 
164 
related enzymes can be divided into three major types according to the target positions of 
165 
enzymes on hemicellulose chains. Exohemicellulases, such as β-D-xylosidase and β-D-
166 
mannosidase, can break the glycosidic bonds at free ends of the hemicellulose chains; 
167 
endohemicellulases, such as β-D-xylanase, β-D-mannanase, can break glycosidic bonds at 
168 
random positions or specific positions; and additional enzymes such as acetyl xylan esterase 
169 
are required to remove side-chain hemicellulose (Franco et al., 2004; Razeq et al., 2018).  
170 
Since both of cellulases and hemicellulases are both responsible catalyzing lignocellulose 
171 
hydrolysis and share some common features, the enzymes were grouped into Carbohydrate-
172 
Active Enzymes (CAZymes) database with some other enzymes with hydrolytic activities for 
173 
carbohydrates such as starch and chitin. They have been further classified into 169 glycosyl 
174 
hydrolases (GHs) and 18 carbohydrate esterases (CEs) families (Lombard et al., 2014) based 
175 
on the nucleic acid or amino acid sequence enzymes’ catalytic modules. Some GHs are 
176 
multifunctional, having high catalytic activity to several kinds of cellulosic substrates. For 
177 
example, the endoglucanase EG5C-1 from Bacillus subtilis BS-5 can hydrolyze both 
178 


[PDF page 11]
9 
carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC) and p-nitrophenyl-β-D-cellobioside at hydrolytic activities of 
179 
4,170 and 2,550 U/μmol, respectively (Zhan et al., 2018). CbXyn10C and Bgxg1 expressed 
180 
from Lactobacillus plantarum also showed hydrolytic activities on xylan, avicel, and CMC 
181 
(Guo et al., 2019). A total of 39 cellulases and hemicellulases can be related to Proteiniphilum 
182 
saccharofermentans M3/6 for the degradation of arabinan, xylan, mannan, and β-glucans 
183 
(Tomazetto et al., 2018). The example illustrates the complexity of cellulolysis enzyme system 
184 
in AD, and the necessity of genomic-based study for understanding such system in the microbes.

2.3 Cellulosomal enzymatic components in AD 
187 
AD is an engineered environment with limited electron acceptors, in which the microbes 
188 
cannot completely oxidize the organic matters for energy. To survive in the critical condition, 
189 
cellulosomes was evolved in anaerobes for effective uptake of the hydrolysate after cellulolysis. 
190 
Cellulosome is a function-specific unit composed of catalytic proteins and non-catalytic 
191 
proteins (Wang et al., 2019). Cellulosomes have been identified on the surface of several 
192 
anaerobes, i.e., most of which are bacteria and some fungi. In different species, cellulosomes 
193 
present with specific molecular arrangement with varying degrees of complexity (Artzi et al., 
194 
2017). An example of cellulosomes formed by Clostridium clariflavum and their assembly 
195 
structure proposed by Artzi et al. (2015) has been illustrated in Figure 4. The non-catalytic 
196 
proteins are referred to scaffoldins (Ding & Bayer, 2020; Ichikawa et al., 2014), which are 
197 
large proteins serving as foundation  of the cellulosomal complex. The functional domains on 
198 
scaffoldins are carbohydrate-binding modules (CBMs) and cohesions (Leis et al., 2017), of 
199 
which CBMs are the protein domains that can tightly bind to the lignocellulose fibers, and 
200 
cohesions contains GHs and CEs as major catalytic proteins for hydrolysis.  The scaffoldins 
201 
can contain up to hundreds of cohesions regions for various specific types of substrates and 
202 
thus the fixed catalytic subunits can continuously conduct hydrolysis.  
203 


[PDF page 12]
10 
Cellulosomes can consist of more than one scaffoldins binding to each other by cohesin-
204 
dockerin. For example, the cellulosome formed by Clostridium thermocellum can have at least 
205 
8 types of scaffoldins (Hong et al., 2014). ScaA is the scaffoldin with CBM, it has type I cohesin 
206 
and type II dockerin (Figure 4) (Bule et al., 2017). It can anchor on the cell surface by 
207 
connecting to ScaF or ScaD with type II dockerin-cohesin connection (Bule et al., 2018). ScaE 
208 
is the cell-free scaffoldin, which can create a large cell-free cellulosome by connecting its type 
209 
II cohesins with type II dockerins of ScaH/L and ScaA. In addition, free uncomplexed enzymes 
210 
and cell-bound cellulosome complexes were produced simultaneously. The structure, 
211 
compositions and corresponding substrates of cellulosomes are regulated and varied by the 
212 
nature of the growth substrates and the host microbes. Artzi et al. (2015) demonstrated that the 
213 
cellulosome proteins produced from Clostridium clariflavum were regulated by the type of 
214 
substrates including cellobiose, microcrystalline cellulose, and acid-pretreated switchgrass. 
215 
The outer membrane receptor protein and sensor may have potential function of carbohydrate-
216 
sensing and CAZyme gene regulation (Kougias et al., 2018). A bioinformatics study for 
217 
screening the cellulosomal modules found that beyond the cellulolysis, 23.6% of the dockerin-
218 
containing proteins were related to alternative biological processes such as protein degradation 
219 
or putative lysozyme activity in the bovine rumen microbiome (Bensoussan et al., 2017). The 
220 
dockerin-cohesin systems in a wide variety of different biological and cellular processes 
221 
implied its possible evolutionary directions and potential application for cellulosomes complex. 
222 
The multi-enzymatic systems could serve as a powerful tool that assists in novel 
223 
biotechnologies. 
224 
In summary, cellulosomes can improve the synergistic efficiency among hydrolytic 
225 
subunits; prevent a single low-efficiency enzyme from adsorbing cellulose and competition 
226 
between different enzymes due to the same absorption site; and have a continuous cellulolysis 
227 


[PDF page 13]
11 
on long-chain lignocellulose. Its modular nature makes it highly efficient in cellulolysis and 
228 
feasible to be artificially manufactured through biotechnology for large-scale applications.

3. Genetic Fingerprint of Cellulolysis 
231 
3.1 Cellulolysis and methanogenesis of anaerobic microbes 
232 
Hydrolytic microbes are key participants of AD. The most predominant bacterial 
233 
hydrolyzers belong to several phyla, i.e., Firmicutes, Bacteroidetes, Proteobacteria, and 
234 
Chloroflexi. The abundances of those hydrolytic bacteria are related to the process conditions 
235 
such as organic loading, solids retention time (SRT), temperature, and types of substrates (Chen 
236 
et al., 2016; Nguyen et al., 2019b). Meanwhile, it is difficult to estimate the taxonomic 
237 
borderline of hydrolyzers, acidogens and acetogens. For example, more than hydrolysis of 
238 
cellulose, some hydrolyzers such as Clostridium cellulolyticum can further oxidize the glucose 
239 
and produce acetate H2 and CO2 (Jia et al., 2018). To understand the niche of those 
240 
multifunction players may help to design the system with better robustness and stability.  
241 
Anaerobic hydrolytic fungi belong to phylum Neocallimastigomycota. Compared to the 
242 
hydrolytic bacteria, hydrolytic fungi have rhizoid system which provides ability of mechanical 
243 
disruption of the lignocellulose tissues through invasive growth (Dollhofer et al., 2015). 
244 
Neocallimastigomycota are also the only fungi forming cellulosomes, of which the genes 
245 
encoding cellulosomal enzyme may be obtained from hydrolytic bacteria through horizontal 
246 
gene transfer (Murphy et al., 2019). Although with some imperfection such as low abundance 
247 
in system and easy to be wash out, anaerobic fungi have great potential in engineering 
248 
application. Fungal bioaugmentation for enhancing cellulolysis in AD increased the methane 
249 
yield by 15-33% for treating cereal crops/straws and cow manure (Akyol et al., 2019). Using 
250 
immobilized fungi in AD increased the methane yields of batch and continuous flow system 
251 
by 50% (from around 250 ml/g VSS increased to around 375 ml/g VSS) and 13-31% (from 
252 


[PDF page 14]
12 
around 600 (Day 0 to Day 10) and 4,200 ml/d (Day 70 to Day 90) increased to around 680 and 
253 
5300 ml/d, respectively), respectively (Guo et al., 2021).

3.2 Genome-wide analysis of cellulosome system and cellulosomics 
256 
Fundamental cellulosome study has been conducted after a comprehensive and time-
257 
consuming procedure. The most accurate way to study the enzyme system is by cultivating 
258 
specific microbes for pure enzyme analysis. However, only a small fraction of anaerobic 
259 
bacteria can be grown in the laboratory. The current understanding of cellulosomes were 
260 
thereafter limited only within a finite number of bacteria strains. With the rapid development 
261 
of novel sequencing technology, nevertheless, new perspectives for deciphering the complex 
262 
and ingenious cellulolysis system are constructed. The gene-centric cellulosome systematic 
263 
study approach can now screen and discover unknown CAZymes, dockerin-cohesin systems, 
264 
signal/regulation system, and other cellulosome-relative active substance (Smith et al., 2017). 
265 
Metagenome, whole-genome, and transcriptome sequencing approaches have been widely 
266 
used to investigate the cellulolytic microbes and cellulosome relative proteins.  
267 
Metagenomics and transcriptomics study provide powerful tools in cellulolysis research. 
268 
AD is a mix-culture microbial system and metagenome information can provide immediate 
269 
insights of the major participants and the potential metabolism pathway of those 
270 
microorganisms. Transcriptomics analysis focuses on mRNA analysis of microbes, which is 
271 
an index of gene expression (Chen et al., 2017). It is the tool to compare different gene 
272 
expressions among distinct cell populations, or the gene expressions of a cell population 
273 
response to different environmental conditions. It is also a useful method to identify the 
274 
metabolism pathways, the cell signaling system, and the gene regulation models in biological 
275 
systems. For cellulosome study, transcriptomics has been applied to observe the changes in 
276 
gene expression of an engineered C. thermocellum strain as a response to change carbon 
277 


[PDF page 15]
13 
sources from xylose to cellobiose (Tafur Rangel et al., 2020). Kougias et al. (2018) conducted 
278 
a shotgun metagenomics analysis on the AD treating pig manure and meadow grass.  A total 
279 
of 151 genomes were recovered by binning process, while 80 of which were completely new 
280 
and 
cannot 
be 
found 
in 
existing 
database. 
Uncultured 
members 
of 
281 
Bacteroidetes and Firmicutes were indicated to play important roles for polysaccharide 
282 
degradation, among which the Firmicutes species, the number and functional roles of CBMs 
283 
of cellulosomes were different.  Wilkens et al. (2017) identified 19,335 CAZymes genes from 
284 
four full-scale mesophilic AD treating sludge in municipal wastewater treatment plants by 
285 
metagenomic sequencing, A total of 10,374 genes belonged to 133 GH families and 2,064 
286 
belonged to 17 CE families. However, only 54% of the CAZymes genes were annotated with 
287 
its function and the others can only be classified into one enzyme family. The results indicated 
288 
that our knowledge of the CAZy subfamilies are limited, while more comprehensive database 
289 
is to be developed especially for the AD treating lignocellulosic biomass. 
290 
Potential RsgI-like carbohydrate sensing proteins were identified with a group of 
291 
upregulated xylose hydrolytic enzymes. In addition, the candidate ABC transporters for xylose 
292 
transport and assimilation were also revealed in this transcriptomics study. By transcriptomic 
293 
analysis, Raman et al. (2011) observed in later stages of cellulose fermentation with C. 
294 
thermocellum ATCC 27405 that several cellulosomal catalytic subunits (i.e., mannanases, 
295 
xyloglucanase, pectate lyase, etc., belongs to GH families ) were significant upregulated for 
296 
degrading non-cellulolytic substrates. Increasing expression of non-cellulolytic enzymes in the 
297 
free cellulosomes during the latter stage of growing might be applicable for exposing the 
298 
cellulose from untapped resources. The protease inhibiting components were also expressed 
299 
progressively, which might protect the cellulosome from protease driven degradation.  
300 
At present, the sequencing technologies have been updated rapidly. The third-generation 
301 
sequencing platforms such as PacBio Sequel and Oxford Nanopore are able to generate more 
302 


[PDF page 16]
14 
accurate sequence reads with enhanced information like DNA methylation signals. The 
303 
decreasing sequencing costs would also allow researchers to pay more attention on 
304 
cellulosomics. The extension of the genetic information database coupling with more analytical 
305 
tools will definitely help to understand and utilize the cellulosome system in AD.

4. Adjustment of Reaction Kinetics in Considering Cellulolysis 
308 
4.1 Micro-aeration for enhancing biodegradability of lignocellulosic biomass in AD 
309 
Microaerobic digestion has gained increased attention in recent years. This process refers 
310 
to the addition of a small well-controlled amount of air or oxygen into the AD. Although 
311 
oxygen has long been considered as an inhibitor to obligate anaerobes, many studies have 
312 
demonstrated improved digestibility and stability of AD processes under microaerobic 
313 
conditions (Botheju & Bakke, 2011; Nguyen & Khanal, 2018). The merits of this operational 
314 
approach include improved methane production, enhanced enzymatic hydrolysis, release of 
315 
VFA accumulation, and mitigation of hydrogen sulfide, which facilitate the overall 
316 
performances of the AD systems (Charles et al., 2009; Fdz-Polanco et al., 2009; Johansen & 
317 
Bakke, 2006; Lim & Wang, 2013; Nguyen et al., 2019a). Specifically, an oxidation-reduction 
318 
potential (ORP)-based oxygenation approach, where ORP level varies linearly with the 
319 
logarithmic dissolved oxygen (DO) concentration, allows for a more precise control of 
320 
oxygen/air injection far below 0.1 mg/L into a digester (Khanal & Huang, 2003). Given these 
321 
advantages, several research groups have applied such microaerobic strategies for digestion of 
322 
lignocellulosic biomass such as corn/wheat/rice straw, napier grass, cow manure, etc. (Fu et al., 
323 
2016a; Fu et al., 2016b; Nguyen et al., 2019a; Tsapekos et al., 2017; Wang et al., 2020c). Fu 
324 
et al. (2016a; 2016b) reported simultaneous improvements in methane yield and VS removal 
325 
efficiency in batch thermophilic digestions of corn straw either by air pretreatment or by direct 
326 
air injection. While pretreatment obtained 16.24% higher in methane production, direct 
327 


[PDF page 17]
15 
treatment achieved 28.45% higher in methane production and 29.43% higher in VS removal, 
328 
respectively. Nguyen et al. (2019a) developed an ORP-based intermittent microaerobic AD 
329 
system to treat napier grass and achieved a stable methane yield of 80.8±9.6 N CH4 mL/VS g 
330 
when picomolar oxygen was applied by setting the ORP + 25 mV above the strict anaerobic 
331 
baseline every other day.  
332 
The underlying microbial mechanism is that the supply of insufficient oxygen creates a 
333 
unique ecological niche suitable for both anaerobes and aerobes, promoting a new balance 
334 
between hydrolytic bacteria, fermentative bacteria, methanogens, as well as facultative bacteria 
335 
(Nguyen & Khanal, 2018; Nguyen et al., 2019a; Wu et al., 2021). The facultative oxygen 
336 
scavenger may rapidly consume the dissolved oxygen in system, shielding the surrounding 
337 
anaerobic cells from direct oxygen attack. Moreover, facultative anaerobes perform 
338 
thermodynamic advantages over strictly anaerobes, making them grow faster and 
339 
predominance under microaerobic conditions (Nguyen et al., 2019a; Rittmann & McCarty, 
340 
2001). Wu et al. (2021) unveiled the metabolic pathway of an ORP-based microaerobic 
341 
digestion of lignocellulose by genome-centric metagenomics. It confirmed that the cellulolytic 
342 
facultative Proteiniphilum strains repeatedly dominated in microaerobic system, which 
343 
encoded rich extracellular enzymes in lignocellulose hydrolysis, fermentative pathway to 
344 
formate, acetate or propionate, as well as complete aerobic respiration pathway to CO2. 
345 
Meanwhile, the high oxygen affinity bd-type terminal oxidases among oxidative 
346 
phosphorylation pathway in Proteiniphilum strains were assumed to closely associated with 
347 
such picomolar oxygen conditions. Moreover, many microbial community analyses also 
348 
reported that microaeration led to higher abundance and activity of hydrolytic bacteria, 
349 
especially the phylum Firmicutes (Fu et al., 2016a; Lim et al., 2014). In addition, the strictly 
350 
anaerobes (especially the methanogens), as well as facultative bacteria, both have been widely 
351 
found to possess various antioxidant enzymes (e.g., superoxide dismutase (SOD), superoxide 
352 


[PDF page 18]
16 
reductase (SOR), etc.), overcoming the oxidative stress under microaerobic circumstances 
353 
(Imlay, 2002). Microaeration triggers a synergistic relationship between hydrolytic facultative 
354 
heterotrophs and anaerobic methanogens, which facilitate the energetic conversion from 
355 
intermediates to methane in order to maintain an overall stability of AD processes (shown in 
356 
Figure 4, by Nguyen et al. (2019a) and Wu et al. (2021)). 
357 
In application, microaeration significantly relies on the appropriate oxygen injection into 
358 
the digester, which refers to aeration type (air or pure oxygen), method (direct inject, 
359 
electrolytic aeration, etc.), frequency (one time, intermittently, or continuously), and 
360 
application stage (pre-, post-, or simultaneous treatment) (Girotto et al., 2018; González-
361 
González & Cuadros, 2015; Rafieenia et al., 2017). While excess aeration resulted in an 
362 
overwhelming deterioration to AD process (Xu et al., 2014), inadequate aeration also requires 
363 
a new microbial balance in system, which influence the system stability (Zhu et al., 2009). 
364 
Thus, for widely use of microaerobic approach in cellulose digestion, fully understanding of 
365 
cellulose conversion pathway under different microaeration control process are necessary. 
366 
The biological process control and oxygen uptake is a crucial parameter for metabolic 
367 
pathway and system optimization. The oxygen transfer efficiency (OTE) decreases during the 
368 
aeration process because of changing sludge conditions can be quantified by the α-factor, the 
369 
ratio of OTE in the process condition to that in clean water (Metcalf et al., 2014). When 
370 
microaeration is performed as a pretreatment method, the α-factor should be carefully 
371 
monitored because the excess DO could reduce the methane potential, and insufficient oxygen 
372 
could potentially fail to produce the beneficial effect of microaeration  (Girotto et al., 2018). 
373 
The intensity of microaeration measured in terms of aeration or oxygen flow rate is the key 
374 
control parameter determining the effects of microaeration (Nguyen & Khanal, 2018). In the 
375 
microaeration system, high mixed liquor suspended solids (MLSS) could systematically 
376 


[PDF page 19]
17 
decrease the α-factor, and ultra-fine bubble diffusers could suffer from greater oxygen transfer 
377 
depression (i.e., fouling, viscosity, and bubble coalescence) (Garrido-Baserba et al., 2017).  
378 
 
379 
4.2 Pretreatment to improve biodegradability of lignocellulosic biomass before AD 
380 
Integrating pretreatment techniques with AD to improve the hydrolysis efficiency has 
381 
attracted significant attentions for the valorization of lignocellulosic biomass (Xu et al., 2019). 
382 
In biorefinery, pretreatment process has been developed mainly to decompose, migrate, and 
383 
modify the building block chemicals to facilitate the enzyme-substrate interactions (Liu et al., 
384 
2016). The key mechanisms applied in pretreatment process design include biomass 
385 
depolymerization (Li et al., 2020), reduction and form-shifting of the crystalline cellulose in 
386 
the substrate(Ling et al., 2020), breakage of the lignin obstruction (Sabeeh et al., 2020), 
387 
removal of inhibitors (Koupaie et al., 2019), creation of more accessible binding sites for 
388 
hydrolytic enzymes (Kainthola et al., 2019a), and ease the growth of methanogens (Zhen et al., 
389 
2017). Pretreatment is a substrate- and product-specific unit operation, for which significant 
390 
amount of energy, chemicals, and/or other resources (e.g., space) are invested for waste 
391 
valorization (Zhang et al., 2020). Based on the decomposition principles and process conditions, 
392 
the modern pretreatment can be classified into three categories, i.e., physical, chemical, and 
393 
biological processes, which summarized in Table 2 and discussed in the following sections. 
394 
Physical pretreatment aims at increasing the surface area and biomass porosity to increase 
395 
the accessibility of enzyme (Leu & Zhu, 2013) and/or anaerobic microorganism (when 
396 
cellulosome is of concern) to the substrate surface (Wang et al., 2019). It can also reduce the 
397 
viscosity of the slurry substrate for better agitation even under high solid loads (Lu et al., 2020). 
398 
The techniques involve in physical pretreatment include mechanical, thermal, ultrasonic, and 
399 
microwave process (Wang et al., 2020b). The mechanical pretreatment such as milling, 
400 
grinding, and drum rotating are developed to reduce the particle size of substrates and/or 
401 


[PDF page 20]
18 
improve the mixing between the treated solids and liquid (Carrere et al., 2016). Kang et al. 
402 
(2019) reported an increased biodegradability index by 13.5% when the size of hybrid 
403 
Pennisetum biomass decreased from 0.83 mm to 0.25 mm. When the size of wheat straw 
404 
reduced from 50 mm to 2 mm, the methane yield increased from 285 to 334 mL/g VS (Menardo 
405 
et al., 2012). The methane yield also increased 53% when the particle size of Laminariaceae 
406 
spp. Biomass was reduced to 76-836 μm (Tedesco et al., 2014). However, previous studies also 
407 
indicated that, in some occasions, further reduction substrate particle size could result in over 
408 
production of VFAs which inhibited the biogas production (Izumi et al., 2010). Thermal 
409 
pretreatment is typically carried out by pressurized vessel, in which the substrate can be swelled 
410 
under pressure and heat (Banu & Kavitha, 2017; Zhou et al., 2015). Depends on the process 
411 
condition, thermal pretreatment may induce different chemical reactions such as hemicellulose 
412 
hydrolysis, hydrogen bonds breakage in crystalline celluloses, and/or changes in lignin phase 
413 
(Veluchamy & Kalamdhad, 2017). Hydrothermal pretreatment coupled with AD was proven 
414 
to complete removal of hemicellulose, for which the cellulose-enriched biomass can be used 
415 
for biomethane production (Kaur et al., 2019). Biomass treated under mild and eco-friendly 
416 
hydrothermal condition reduced the formation of inhibitors prior to AD (Dasgupta & Chandel, 
417 
2019). Passos et al. (2018) reported a 96% increase of methane yield when coffee husk and 
418 
microalgae were pretreated at 120°C for 60 min. The methane yield was increased by 57.7% 
419 
(344 mL/ g VS) when the wheat straw was pretreated at 180°C for 1 h (Rajput & Visvanathan, 
420 
2018). Microwave treatment is another type of thermal pretreatment of which electromagnetic 
421 
radiation was applied for substrate decomposition at a wide range of frequency and wavelength 
422 
(Marin et al., 2010). This pretreatment process can effectively increase the methane yield of 
423 
AD (Wang et al., 2021), while some negative impacts were reported when it was applied on 
424 
low lignin or lignocellulose substrate such as food or vegetable/fruit wastes (Atelge et al., 2020). 
425 
Ultrasonic pretreatment mechanically disrupts the fiber, cell structure and floc matrix through 
426 


[PDF page 21]
19 
the shear force by the vibration probe (Elliott & Mahmood, 2007). Pilli et al. (2016) applied 
427 
ultrasonic pretreatment (750 w, 20 kHz) on PS and reported an increase of 17.3% higher 
428 
methane yield (273 mL/g VS) than the control study. Ormaechea et al. (2018) found that 
429 
ultrasonic pretreated cattle manure reached to 460 mL/g VS, which was 58.6% higher than the 
430 
control. Microwave and ultrasonic pretreatment are effective but both require high energy and 
431 
maintenance fee when large scale application is of concern (Hassan et al., 2018). 
432 
Chemical pretreatment is a common approach to reduce biomass recalcitrant of 
433 
lignocellulosic biomass in biorefinery (Islam et al., 2020). Acids, alkaline and oxidants have 
434 
been applied under various conditions to destroy or fractionate the complex structure and to 
435 
improve biodegradability of the substrate (Nguyen et al., 2021). Alkaline pretreatment is a 
436 
widely applied strategy for low recalcitrant biomass which can be conducted at ambient room 
437 
temperature and pressure with modest energy consumption and without producing harmful by-
438 
product for downstream process (Behera et al., 2014; Shah, 2018). Alkaline also has the 
439 
function of swelling, and solubilization of COD and other macromolecules, such as 
440 
carbohydrates and protein to increase the digestibility of substrate (Nguyen et al., 2021). You 
441 
et al. (2019) reported an increase of 38.5% methane yield when conducting the co-digestion of 
442 
NaOH and CaO pretreated corn stover and swine manure. Zhu et al. (2020) tested the AD 
443 
performance of wheat straw by using 2.0% alkaline liquor and 1:3 synthetic urine, they 
444 
obtained increase in the methane yield by 68% and 56%, respectively. Acid pretreatment is 
445 
another effective method to break down the lignocellulosic structure (van der Waals force, 
446 
hydrogen bonds and covalent bonds) and for higher AD performance (Mussoline et al., 2013; 
447 
Wada et al., 2010). Dilute acid (DA) pretreatment assists the higher reaction rates of AD and 
448 
improves the susceptibility of cellulose to hydrolysis (Amin et al., 2017). In a study conducted 
449 
by Song et al. (2014), the biodegradation of corn straw was effectively accomplished in all acid 
450 
pretreatments. Among the studied acids (CH3COOH, HCl and H2SO4), the corn straw 
451 


[PDF page 22]
20 
pretreated with 2% H2SO4 obtained the highest methane yield of 175.6 mL/g VS ,which was 
452 
74.6% higher than that of untreated straw. Syaichurrozi et al. (2019) pretreated Salvinia 
453 
molesta biomass by 4% H2SO4 and generated 81.8% higher biogas production with shortened 
454 
lag phase. Saha et al. (2018) achieved a 10.0% increase in methane yield after 0.2 M acetic acid 
455 
pretreatment of mixed fruit waste. However, growth inhibitors could be produced during DA 
456 
pretreatment, such as furfural and hydroxymethylfurfural (HMF) which hinder the AD 
457 
(Jönsson & Martín, 2016).  
458 
In addition to acid and base, oxidants such as hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) has been 
459 
commonly used in pulping and bleaching industries for lignin removal. H2O2 can break down 
460 
the structure of lignocellulose through releasing hydroxyl radicals (e.g., HO· and HOO·) and 
461 
molecular oxygen at low concentration via strong oxidation ability (Mao et al., 2015). Unlike 
462 
alkaline or DA pretreatment, peroxide pretreatment can be used at relatively mild concentration, 
463 
temperature and atmospheric pressure, while effectively removing lignin from various 
464 
lignocellulosic agricultural residues (Cabrera et al., 2014). When Miscanthus floridulus was 
465 
treated by 0.8% H2O2 for 24 h, the methane yield of 279 mL/g VS was achieved, which was 
466 
49% higher than the control assay (Katukuri et al., 2017). Liu et al. (2018) demonstrated that 
467 
peroxidation through H2O2 was a useful method to disintegrate sludge, leading to higher sCOD 
468 
and increased methane production by 19.89% when microwave-H2O2 pretreatment applied.  
469 
Biological pretreatment is considered as environmental-friendly and inexpensive technique 
470 
for AD as this process does not require high energy and chemical inputs for operation (Zhao et 
471 
al., 2018). Previous researches have tried to improve the AD process by using anaerobic or 
472 
aerobic predigestion or enzyme addition (Atelge et al., 2020). The anaerobic pretreatment 
473 
predigests the substrate under mesophilic or thermophilic environments (Neumann et al., 2016). 
474 
For anaerobic pretreatment of sewage sludge, temperature phased anaerobic digestion (TPAD) 
475 
is a commonly applied choice for which a primary or hyper thermophilic digester is applied 
476 


[PDF page 23]
21 
with a secondary mesophilic digester (known as two-stage AD) (Ariunbaatar et al., 2014). A 
477 
recent study performed TPAD on wastewater sludge digestion found a methane release rate of 
478 
3.55 L CH4 /L day at 45°C along with a 77% reduction in VS (Hameed et al., 2019). For fungal 
479 
pretreatment, several classes including brown-rot, white-rot, soft-rot and basidiomycete fungi, 
480 
have been used in pretreatment to increase feedstock solubilization (Shirkavand et al., 2017).  
481 
Enzymes such as peroxidase and laccase excreted  by fungi can directly degrade the lignin and 
482 
increase the digestibility of substrate in AD (Zheng et al., 2014). The pretreatment of rice straw 
483 
by wood-decay fungus Pleurotus ostreatus, Phanerochaete chrysosposrium, and Ganoderma 
484 
lucidum increased 64%, 122% and 88% of gas production, respectively (Kainthola et al., 2019a; 
485 
Kainthola et al., 2019b). Trichoderma reesei and Aspergillus are investigated in many studies 
486 
for its capability to excrete cellulase to degrade the insoluble cellulose that is found in large 
487 
portions in the sludge, food waste and other lignocellulosic biomass (Gerhardt et al., 2007; 
488 
Nguyen et al., 2021). The effects of lysozyme pretreatments on WAS hydrolysis and 
489 
degradability was evaluated, and Chen et al. (2018) found that lysozymes can increase the 
490 
sCOD concentration in the sludge by 2.23 times and 2.15 times higher than protease and α-
491 
amylase. The sludge flocculation disintegration was also improved. Comparing with the non-
492 
biological pretreatment, biological pretreatment generates no toxic compounds (e.g., phenolic 
493 
compounds), production of potential useful by-products, milder operation condition and low 
494 
cost for waste disposal which is more compatible with AD (Wagner et al., 2018; Wu & He, 
495 
2013). 
496 
While demonstrated to be effective in biomass fractionation, the increased capital and 
497 
operational costs of different pretreatment processes have hindered the large-scale application 
498 
of biorefinery. Most of the pretreated substrates are free from microorganisms and ready to be 
499 
utilized by pure enzyme or single strain. The pretreatment techniques need to apply with 
500 
specific targets with the production extremely high value products to achieve a more 
501 


[PDF page 24]
22 
economical operation of AD. Figure 6 illustrates the trades of various parameters applied in 
502 
AD among increased costs from pretreatment, changed methane yield, and extended time of 
503 
operation. The overall operational costs of enzymatic pretreatment are lower in the report due 
504 
to its extremely low energy and chemical consumption in physical and chemical pretreatment 
505 
processes. However, the significant limitation on the application of the biological pretreatment 
506 
are of its long reaction time and more sensitive responses to the ambient environment (Sindhu 
507 
et al., 2016). The requirement of long SRT operation reflected into large land requirement has 
508 
prevented a wide application of biological pretreatment in urbanized areas, while also increase 
509 
the difficulty of process control and operation.

5. Perspectives 
512 
AD has demonstrated to be a useful selection for the conversion of pretreated 
513 
lignocellulosic substrate and high strength cellulosic waste. With the advances of new 
514 
generation genomic tools, the dynamic changes of the microbial ecology have been gradually 
515 
clarified, which has been demonstrated in the example of cellulosome-induced-cellulolysis in 
516 
this review. Co-digestion of different feedstock, i.e., FW, AW, and/or cellulosic sludge, with 
517 
activated sludge have been attracting significant attentions in large-scale practices in recent 
518 
years. However, recent publications on the optimization of substrate-sludge combinations for 
519 
specific AD process may not represent the total production rates of various waste sources. More 
520 
advanced technique and understanding will therefore soon become mandatory for a broad range 
521 
of AD research, i.e., from process design/operation to genetic modification. By clarifying the 
522 
dockerin-cohesin systems in AD, more effective hydrolyzing tools may be synthesized from 
523 
wastewater, which benefits cellulose valorization in multi-substrate treatment.  
524 
For process upgrade, microaeration offers significant benefits to cellulolysis-methanogens 
525 
interactions in AD, while innovative gas diffusion process, real-time monitoring principles, 
526 


[PDF page 25]
23 
and control techniques are to be investigated in order to optimize the DOTE in the high solid 
527 
loading environment. The potential residual oxygen in the solid layers and headspace in large 
528 
scale operation needs to be carefully designed with minimum energy inputs, gas flow, and fire 
529 
risks. In addition, pretreatment techniques of lignocellulosic biomass has demonstrated its role 
530 
before bioconversion. Physical, chemical, and biological processes have claimed their niches 
531 
in different beneficial areas among conversion efficiency, product values, and costs, 
532 
respectively. While harsh pretreatment may better facilitate the biomass recalcitrant for 
533 
bioconversion, the “bacteria-free” nature of pretreated substrate leads to an AD (multi-culture) 
534 
over saccharification (cellulase)/fermentation (single culture) discussion on how to utilize the 
535 
easily degradable substrates for most valuable products. Future development of the 
536 
pretreatment/AD processes may consider the joint mechanisms applying the energy and/or 
537 
recycled bioconversion metabolites (e.g., waste heat, ammonia, organic acid, and other 
538 
fermentation products) for the development of integrated processes. Separation techniques for 
539 
treated solid digestate are as importance as conventionally believed for balancing the product 
540 
values from AD with various critical SRTs to be investigated. 
541 
 
542 
6. Conclusions 
543 
Hydrolysis serves as the rate limiting factor of AD receiving lignocellulosic and cellulosic 
544 
needs to be accelerated by various approaches toward a sustainable bio-economy. This critical 
545 
review summarized the key biological factors when applying AD for the valorisation of 
546 
cellulosic biomass waste, including biodegradabilities of lignocellulose biomass, functional 
547 
microorganisms of cellulolysis, their functional enzyme category, and cellulosomes. The 
548 
genetic fingerprint of cellulolysis, engineering application of micro-aeration with its inherent 
549 
genetics changes and pretreatment methods for lignocellulose biomass are also reviewed.
