Abstract 
This study investigated the performance of combined nanobubble water (NW) and 
digestate in the soaking hydrolysis process. Two types of NW (CO2NW and O2NW) 
with digestate were used to soak rice straw for 1, 2, 3, 5, and 7 days. During soaking 
process, the volatile fatty acids (VFA) concentration in the treatment with O2NW and 
digestate for 3 days (O2NW-3 d) reached 7179.5 mg-HAc/L. Moreover, the highest 
specific methane yield (SMY) obtained in this treatment could reach 336.7 NmL/gVS. 
Although the addition of NW did not significantly increase SMY from digestate 
soaking, NW could accelerate the rate of methane production and reduce digestion time 
of T80. The enrichment of Enterobacter in the soaking process was observed when using 
CO2NW and O2NW as soaking solutions which played important roles in VFA 
production. This study provides a new insight into environment-friendly enhanced crop 
straw pretreatment, combining NW and digestate soaking hydrolysis. 
 
Keywords: Soaking pretreatment; Nanobubbles; Biogas production; Microbial 
community.

1. Introduction 
Hydrolysis is usually the limit step of the anaerobic digestion (AD) process, 
especially for lignocellulosic materials (Wang et al., 2020). Hence, the pre-hydrolysis 
processes of crop straw, such as ensiling (Sun et al., 2019) or short-term digestate 
soaking in the tank (Wei et al., 2020), are commonly employed as biological 
pretreatment methods due to the technical feasibility and cost-effectiveness. 
In recent years, nanobubble water (NW), with bubble sizes of less than 1000 nm in 
diameter (ISO 20480:2017), has attracted increasing interest in the AD process. Up to 
now, some studies have shown that NW could enhance the AD process by strengthening 
key microorganisms and enzyme activity (Chuenchart et al., 2021). This enhancement 
was attributed to the facilitation of efficient mass transfer of gases and nutrients due to 
small size and large surface area of nanobubbles (Wang et al., 2020). In addition, some 
studies reported that the addition of small amounts of O2 into anaerobic reactors was 
beneficial for the growth of facultative hydrolytic and acidogenic microorganisms (Wu 
et al., 2021). Bajón Fernández et al. (2015) reported that CO2 enrichment contributed to 
promoting the production of acetic acid via the Wood-Ljungdahl pathway. 
However, these studies mainly focus on the effect of O2NW and CO2NW addition 
on the one-step AD process. Lack of study on the influence of NW on the pre-hydrolysis 
(soaking) process before AD. In addition, Wei et al. (2020) reported that digestate 
soaking, a biological pretreatment, could increase volatile fatty acid (VFA) production 
by providing abundant microorganisms. This study hypothesized that combining NW 


[PDF page 4]
 
4 
 
(O2NW or CO2NW) and digestate might lead to a favorable hydrolysis effect. 
Therefore, this study investigated the combined impact of NW with digestate on 
the hydrolysis process of rice straw. The main objectives include: (1) investigating the 
performance of O2NW and CO2NW on hydrolysis and acidification during soaking; (2) 
evaluating the kinetics of their effects on methane yield; (3) elucidating the change in 
the microbial community during soaking. This study aimed to provide new insights into 
the combining NW and digestate soaking pretreatment of rice straw. 
2. Materials and methods 
2.1. Rice straw, inoculum, and nanobubble water 
The indica rice straw was collected from a field in Hubei, China, air-dried for 
several days, mechanically chopped, and then sieved through a 20-mesh screen. The 
digestate was taken from a stable mesophilic AD reactor at a sewage treatment facility 
in Beijing. The inoculum-1 for soaking pretreatment was prepared by centrifuging 
(1500 rpm for 50 min) the collected digestate to remove the supernatant by a pilot-scale 
centrifuge (JDR-80, Jiaderen Co., Ltd., China). The inoculum-2 for batch anaerobic 
digestion experiment was prepared by cultivating the digestate for one week until no 
biogas production. 
CO2NW and O2NW were generated by mixing gas and deionized water (DW) in 
nanobubble rapid generation device (TL-MBG10-A, China). The preparation time for 
CO2NW was 10 min and for O2NW was 15 min (Wang et al., 2023). 


[PDF page 5]
 
5 
 
2.2. Digestate and nanobubble soaking pretreatment 
To address the problem of less epiphytic bacteria and low hydrolysis efficiency of 
the rice straw, this experiment aimed to improve soaking pretreatment by adding NW 
and digestate. The inoculum-1, which had a higher total solids (TS) content, could 
potentially address the issue of low reactor volume utilization efficiency in NW-based 
AD. The rice straw and inoculum-1 were loaded into 250-mL glass bottles as a ratio of 
4:1 based on volatile solids (VS). Then the soaking solution (DW, CO2NW, and O2NW) 
was added at a ratio of soaking solution to rice straw 10:1 based on wet weight. Those 
treatments were labeled as DW, CO2NW, and O2NW, respectively. The soaking time 
was set at intervals of 1, 2, 3, 5, and 7 days. The bottles were blown for 1 min with N2, 
sealed with a nitrile plug, and placed in a water bath at 37 ± 1℃. 
2.3. Batch anaerobic digestion 
After the soaking pretreatment, the inoculum-2 was loaded to produce biogas as a 
ratio of 1:1.5 with rice straw, based on VS. Those treatments were also labeled as DW, 
CO2NW, and O2NW, respectively. The untreated rice straw was loaded with the same 
ratio (labeled as untreated). In addition, the biogas yield from the inoculum-2 alone was 
also measured. The bottles were blown and sealed again. Every bottle was placed in a 
water bath at 37 ± 1℃ and manually shaken daily to ensure fermentation uniformity. 
2.4. Analytical method 
The standard methods were used to measure TS and VS (APHA, 2005). The 
cellulose, hemicellulose, and acid detergent lignin contents of the samples were 


[PDF page 6]
 
6 
 
determined according to the method of Van Soest (1963). The VFA concentrations in the 
soaking liquid were filtered through a 0.22 μm microfiber filter and then subjected to 
gas chromatography (GC-2010 Plus, Shimadzu, Japan) analysis. The pH of the liquid 
was measured by a pH meter (FE28, Mettler-Toledo, Switzerland). The composition of 
biogas was determined using gas chromatography (GC8A, Shimadzu, Japan). The 
biogas volume was normalized to standard temperature and pressure conditions after 
being tested by a glass syringe. 
2.5. Microbial community analysis 
The samples were collected from treatments after 1, 3, and 5 days of soaking in 
different soaking solutions to analyze the microbial community. Following the 
manufacturer's instructions, DNA was extracted from samples using an E.Z.N.A.® Soil 
DNA Kit (Omega Bio-tek, Norcross, GA, U.S.). The DNA was amplified with primers 
338F and 806R. The sequence was performed by an Illumina platform device (Illumina, 
San Diego, USA). The procedures for high-throughput sequencing of 16S rRNA were 
cited to an earlier study (Sun et al., 2019). The raw sequencing data were submitted to 
the Sequence Read Archive (SRA) of NCBI with project number PRJNA1056946. 
3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Effect of adding nanobubble water on soaking pretreatment 
3.1.1 VFA production and pH 
VFA served as essential intermediate metabolites in the AD process, and a greater 
concentration of VFA corresponds to an enhanced hydrolysis effect during soaking. The 


[PDF page 7]
 
7 
 
VFA concentration of rice straw soaked in different types of soaking solution (DW, 
CO2NW, and O2NW) combined with digestate is shown in Fig. 1a. The VFA 
concentrations in the O2NW treatments on days 1, 2, 3 were 3062.1 ± 174.8, 5222.4 ± 
864.7, and 7179.5 ± 337.5 mg-HAc/L, which exhibited a significant increase of 22.2%, 
8.5%, and 14.0%, respectively, compared with DW treatments at same soaking time (p 
< 0.05). Likewise, after adding CO2NW for days 1, 2, and 3, it showed the increases of 
16.9%, 7.6%, and 9.9%, respectively, compared with DW treatments (p < 0.05). Those 
results indicated that NW on digestate soaking pretreatment could promote VFA 
production. The pH changes of straw soaked with different water and digestate are 
shown in Fig. 1b. The pH values of DW, CO2NW, and O2NW treatments decreased 
rapidly from 7.67 ± 0.04, 7.67 ± 0.05 and 7.59 ± 0.04 on the 1st day to 5.98 ± 0.20, 5.81 
± 0.01 and 5.73 ± 0.08 on the 3rd day, respectively. However, after 3 days of soaking, 
the change of pH gradually slowed down. 
The O2NW treatment exhibited the most significant increase in VFA yield after 
soaking for 1 day. However, with prolonged soaking time, the rate of VFA increase in 
the NW-added treatment compared with the DW treatment gradually slowed down. This 
might be related to the high VFA concentration, causing a pH decrease, which might 
have a negative effect for acid-producing microorganisms (Fan et al., 2021). 
Furthermore, due to the batch soaking used in this experiment, NW could only be added 
once during loading, potentially leading to limited effects on the pretreatment. However, 
if NW is generated in situ within the AD reactor, continuous supply with NW may hold 


[PDF page 8]
 
8 
 
tremendous potential for lignocellulose pretreatment, especially by adding O2NW. 
3.1.2 Substrate degradation 
The TS degradation rate of straw pretreated with soaking solution and digestate are 
shown in Fig. 1c. The TS degradation rates of CO2NW and O2NW treatments on day 1 
(15.4% and 15.6%) and day 3 (18.5% and 19.4%) were higher than those of DW 
treatments (the TS degradation rates of DW on day 1 and day 3 were 15.1% and 17.6%, 
respectively). When the soaking time was extended for 5 days and 7 days, the TS 
degradation rate of the O2NW treatments was still higher than that of the DW 
treatments. However, there was no significant difference in TS degradation rate between 
CO2NW and DW treatments after soaking for 7 days. 
3.2. Effect of methane yield after soaking pretreatment with 
nanobubble water 
After soaking with NW and digestate for rice straw, the specific methane yield 
(SMY) of each treatment is shown in Fig. 1d. The highest SMY after soaking 
pretreatment was shown in the O2NW-3 d treatment with 336.7 ± 3.1 NmL/gVS, which 
represented an increase of 11.6% compared with the untreated group (301.7 ± 1.5 
NmL/gVS). However, when considering the impact of NW alone, the SMY from the 
O2NW-3 d treatment was not significantly increased compared with the DW-3 d 
treatment (328.4 ± 3.7 NmL/gVS). Although the results exhibited that NW soaking with 
digestate could increase the methane production, the impact of sole NW was minimal. 
When comparing treatments with different soaking times but using the same 


[PDF page 9]
 
9 
 
solution (DW, CO2NW, or O2NW), the SMY showed a significant increase before day 3 
(p < 0.05). As the soaking time was extended for 7 days, the methane production among 
different treatments showed a trend towards consistency. Although O2NW had a higher 
TS degradation rate, there was no significant increase in methane production. As 
reported by Zhang et al. (2016), prolonged soaking time resulted in more organic matter 
loss, including the generation of CO2, H2, and CH4. Therefore, soaking in O2NW for 3 
days was an optimal condition for pretreating rice straw to minimize soaking loss and 
maximize methane production. 
Additionally, the SMY of the CO2NW-1 d treatment was 330.8 ± 3.6 NmL/gVS, 
which was higher compared with the O2NW-1 d treatment (Fig. 1d). This means that the 
soaking efficiency with CO2NW was faster than with O2NW after 1-day soaking. Due to 
its higher solubility compared with other gases, CO2 nanobubbles dissolved gradually to 
form carbonic acid, which could partially dissociate to provide H+, creating an acidic 
environment for the soaking system (Guo et al., 2019). However, the organic acids 
production in other treatments also caused a decrease in pH of the soaking liquid. 
Therefore, as the soaking time was prolonged, the SMY of CO2NW treatment did not 
significantly increase. 
In this study, the modified Gompertz model was employed to fit the SMY of rice 
straw soaked with digestate and NW. For a 3-day soaking period, the lag phase reduced 
from 1.9 days in the untreated group to 0.2 to 0.5 days after soaking. This means that 
soaking pretreatment could shorten the lag period (p < 0.05). In addition, Rm (18.6–19.1

10 
 
NmL/gVS·d) of each treatment after 3-day soaking was higher than that of the untreated 
group (16.5 NmL/gVS·d), particularly in treatments with added NW. This indicated that 
soaking with NW could accelerate the rate of methane production. The digestion time of 
T80 was decreased by 6.6%–16.6% after NW soaking compared with untreated. These 
results showed that soaking pretreatment with NW could accelerate biogas production 
and reduce digestion time. 
3.3. Effect of bacterial community during soaking pretreatment with 
nanobubble water 
The bacterial community after soaking for 1, 3, and 5 days with NW and digestate 
are shown in Fig. 2. It was obvious that Enterobacter and Enterococcus dominated in all 
treatments. Enterococcus, a common Firmicutes in acidic silage, dominated the first day 
of soaking; while Enterobacter, a Proteobacteria phylum, played a significant role in 
converting polysaccharides to acetic acid (Wang et al., 2019). With prolonged soaking, 
Enterococcus was gradually replaced by Enterobacter, which was better suitable for 
lower pH. The reason was that Enterococcus was a coccus, and its ability to adapt to 
harsh environments (low pH) was not as strong as Enterobacter (rod-shaped bacteria) 
(Wang et al., 2019). Interestingly, CO2NW and O2NW treatment exhibited a faster 
replaced trend, indicating that NW accelerated microbial activity. According to the 
comparison of the relative abundance of norank_c_D8A-2, it was observed that NW 
accelerated the elimination of original bacteria of inoculum. The relative abundance of 
Acetomicrobium gradually decreased which was related to the decrease in the pH of 


[PDF page 11]
 
11 
 
soaking liquid after prolonged time. However, O2NW treatment still had a higher 
abundance of Acetomicrobium after soaking 3 d. Fan et al. (2021) reported that airNW-
based AD system had the ability to relieve acid inhibition. The higher relative 
abundance of Clostridium_sensu_stricto_1 in the O2NW-1d treatment compared with 
the other treatments. However, its relative abundance decreased after soaking 3 days, 
meaning that the pH dropped below 6 inhibiting butyric acid production (Lv et al., 
2022). 
Differences in soaking water type and duration resulted in differing pH among 
treatments, directly leading to changes in microbial communities. The VFA produced 
from the degradation of crop straw caused the pH to decrease. Higher VFA 
concentrations indicated better pretreatment effectiveness. But the decrease in pH could 
also inhibit or even cause death to acid-producing bacteria that were sensitive to harsh 
environments, which was unfavorable for the continuous production of VFA. Therefore, 
further research is needed to investigate the addition of buffering agents to maintain an 
appropriate pH, and enhance the activity of hydrolytic acidogenic bacteria. 
Recent studies have reported that nanobubble could regulate key metabolic 
processes through interactions with biomolecular compounds of cells (Marcelino et al., 
2023). On one hand, due to its small size and relative stability, nanobubble enhances the 
efficiency of transporting nutrients and gases (Wang et al., 2020). On the other hand, 
nanobubble could generate reactive oxygen species, which might be detrimental and 
beneficial for microorganisms (Chuenchart et al., 2021; Wu et al., 2021). Currently, the 


[PDF page 12]
 
12 
 
detailed mechanisms of how nanobubble interacts with microorganisms remain 
unknown. Further investigation is needed to clarify whether nanobubble traverses into 
the cell, thus promoting or inhibiting the functions of microorganisms. 
4. Conclusion 
The effect of CO2NW and O2NW addition on improving soaking hydrolysis of rice 
straw was investigated. The SMY in digestate soaking with O2NW for 3 d increased by 
11.6% compared with the untreated group. But NW did not significantly increase SMY 
compared with DW soaking pretreatment. After 3-day of soaking, the VFA 
concentration of O2NW treatment reached 7179.5 mg-HAc/L, and the TS degradation 
rate was 19.4%. Furthermore, the application of CO2NW and O2NW could accelerate 
the enrichment of Enterobacter. This study proposed that NW could enhance soaking 
hydrolysis effect and then accelerate subsequent biogas production by accelerating the 
microbial activity.
