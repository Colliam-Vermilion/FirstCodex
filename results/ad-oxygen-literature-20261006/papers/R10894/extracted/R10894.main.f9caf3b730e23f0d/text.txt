Abstract
Here we comprehensively review the available knowledge on effects of different pretreatment technologies on microbial 
population and microbial dynamics in anaerobic digestion (AD) fed with different substrates and different operational 
parameters. To identify peer-reviewed studies published in English-language journals, a comprehensive search was per­
formed across multiple electronic databases. The eligible studies were analyzed to extract data and information pertaining 
to the configuration of anaerobic reactors, operational parameters, and various pretreatment processes such as chemical, 
biological, enzymatic, thermal, microaerobic, and ultrasonic. The findings derived from this current review demonstrated 
that different chemical, biological, and physical pretreatment technologies improve the biomethane potential (BMP) and 
potentially affect the dominant bacteria and archaea. Moreover, although hydrogenotrophic methanogenesis are more 
observed due to resistance to extreme conditions, methane production follows both aceticlastic and hydrogenotrophic 
pathways in AD assisted with different pretreatment process. Firmicutes and Bacteroidetes phyla of bacteria were the 
dominant hydrolytic bacteria due to synergetic effects of different pretreatment process on solubilization and bioavailabil­
ity of recalcitrant substrates. In summary, a holistic understanding on bacteria and archaea communities, along with the 
mechanisms of the dominant microorganisms leads to enhanced stability and overall performance of anaerobic digestion 
(AD) processes.

Literature search and inclusion criteria


[{"type": "pdf_block", "pdf_page": 2, "pdf_page_label": "440", "block": 5, "bbox_points": [306.1416931152344, 110.09993743896484, 546.8117065429688, 486.0699462890625]}]
The present study was performed to address the main ques­
tion: Do pretreatment technologies influence the dominant 
bacteria and archaea in AD process fed with different substrate 
and different operational parameters?. In order to achieve this 
objective, the researchers utilized the Preferred Reporting 
Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 
guidelines to guide the study design, search strategy, and 
study selection process [24]. Moreover, a comprehensive 
search was conducted across four prominent global electronic 
databases (Scopus, Web of Science, PubMed, and Embase) 
following their respective search guidelines to ensure the 
inclusion of relevant studies. Furthermore, for the rigorous 
selection process of reliable studies, Boolean operators ‘AND’ 
and ‘OR’ were used in conjunction with the following search 
terms: “Microbial community” OR “microbial population” 
OR “microbial diversity” OR “microbial dynamic*” AND 
“anaerobic digestion” OR “anaerobic reactor*”. The compre­
hensive search strategy for different pretreatment process and 
their effects on microbial dynamics and microbial population 
are summarized in additional file. Moreover, the handsearch­
ing was used to identify the additional sources. The detailed 
information on the study selection process and reliability are 
reported in our previous study [9]. We included studies that 
were published in peer-reviewed journals and written in the 
English language, without any restrictions on the publication 
date. The selected studies encompassed microbial variations 
observed in full-scale, lab-scale, and experimental anaerobic 
bioreactors, regardless of the bioreactor configurations. How­
ever, conference papers, book chapters, and abstracts were 
excluded from our analysis.

different pretreatment technologies [9, 22, 23]. However,

microbial population and dominant microorganisms in AD 
assisted with pretreatment technologies. Therefore, a com­

systematic review was designed for the first time to com­
prehensively survey roles of different chemical, biological,

particular emphasis on dominant bacteria and archaea, were 
extracted and systematically organized in appropriate tables.

the pretreatment processes of anaerobic digestion aims to 
enhanced solubilization, methane production, sludge stabiliza­
tion, resource recovery and treatment [2]. However, extracel­

and microbial cell membrane are the most known obstacles 
which inhibit the microbial activity in anaerobic digestion [85]. 
Ultrasound (US) as a physical pretreatment due to cavitation 
mechanism can improve the biodegradability of organic mat­
ters in biological reactors. In US pretreatment, the temperature 
increase to 5000 K and the microbubble with ability of pro­
duction highly reactive OH radicals were generated following 
some consequent stages: growth bubble until unstable diam­
eter and the collapse. In addition, the other beneficial effects of 
US are enzyme extraction, pollutant removal and intracellular 
material recovery [86]. Generally, according to frequency, US 
are divided into three regions: power ultrasound (20–100 kHz), 
high frequency ultrasound (100 kHz– 1 MHz) and diagnostic 
ultrasound (1–500 MHz) [87]. However, according to open lit­
