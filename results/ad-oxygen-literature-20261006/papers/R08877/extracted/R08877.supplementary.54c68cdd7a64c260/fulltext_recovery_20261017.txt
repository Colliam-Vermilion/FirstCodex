To calculate the oxygen load in the DAF system the following assumptions were made:
34
-
No oxygen escapes the system (Qgas0). This would be the worse case scenario in which 
35
the anaerobic biomass in the AD would be subjected to the highest amount of oxygen.
36
-
Air is assumed to be composed of 21% oxygen and 79% nitrogen.
37
-
Dissolution of air at the gicen pressure (or air transfer efficiency) is assumed to be at 70% 
38
in accordance to Wang, et al. [2].
39
-
Dissolved oxygen and nitrogen were calculated based on Henry’s constants for oxygen and 
40
nitrogen (1.2 ×10-3 and 6.5 ×10-4, respectively) and correcting for the desire temperature

[3].

279
To further understand the reasons behind the high methane concentration in the biogas, the effect 
280
of pH in the dissolved concentrations of total inorganic carbon produced (TIC), HCO3-, CO2, and 
281
CO32-, was assessed in the PhreeqC model. The total dissolved carbon concentration from these 
282
three species increased by around 3% in the MA-AnMBR compared to the AnMBR period. At a 
283
pH of 7.6, the MA-AnMBR had a dissolved carbon concentration of 0.42 g∙L-1, while at a pH of 
284
7.4 it was 0.41 g∙L-1 for the AnMBR. The most significant change was observed for carbonic 
285
acid, where the model predicted an HCO3-1 concentration of 2.03 g∙L-1 for the MA-AnMBR state 
286
and 1.92 g∙L-1 for the AnMBR. Moreover, dissolved CO2 concentration was also assessed at a 
287
neutral pH, and results showed that for both periods, the dissolved CO2 concentration was 
288
0.35 g∙L-1. This value could also be linked to the predicted increase of CO2 concentration in the 
289
biogas at pH 7, which was around 14 % for both reactor states. 
290
Aside from an increase in the total inorganic carbon produced, the high acid neutralization 
291
capacity (ANC) of the AnMBR and MA-AnMBR could be linked to the urea concentration of 
292
the feed. A concentration of 1.0 g∙L-1 of urea was added to the synthetic feed. Urea has a molar 
293
mass of 60 g.mol-1, and each mmol of urea is responsible for producing two meq of ammonia 
294
and one of carbon dioxide. At the lab-scale reactor pH, ammonia and carbon dioxide will be 
295
mainly in the form of NH4+ and HCO3-, obtaining a surplus of 17 meq of NH4+ cations. Most of 
296
the feed’s COD content comes from sodium acetate and cellulose, added in concentrations of 2.6 
297
and 1.5 g∙L-1. The first one uses its own sodium as a buffer, and most of the sodium acetate is 
298
converted to methane and NaHCO3 [4]. The produced CO2 from cellulose will be chemically 
299
bound with the surplus of NH4+ cations coming from urea. This will decrease the CO2 content in 
300
biogas, (increasing the partial content of CH4), and improve the system buffer capacity, raising 


[PDF page 12]
301
the ANC/TIC ratio. A rise in this ratio increases the HCO3- concentration in the liquid and 
302
decreases the biogas CO2 concentration [5]. Therefore, the high methane concentration of the 
303
biogas for the AnMBR and MA-AnMBR could be attributed to the feed characteristics, high urea 
304
concentration, and CO2 dissolution into the liquid.
305
Results of the PhreeqC model regarding methane and carbon dioxide biogas concentrations were 
306
further tested with the influent and reactor conditions given by Ozgun, et al. [6], which uses urea 
307
as one of the main sources of ammonium. For the lab-scale reactor operated at a pH of 7, Ozgun, 
308
et al. obtained a CH4 concentration of 61 ± 5.7%, while the model predicted a methane 
309
concentration of 61%. Moreover, for the same conditions, at a pH of 7.65, the results of the 
310
model showed an expected biogas methane concentration of 88%.

SUPPORTING INFORMATION G
339
Methods for microbial community analysis
340

DNA extraction
341
Three triplicate sludge samples were taken to perform microbial population analysis, one 
342
from the AnMBR state (after 150 days of operation), and two from the MA-AnMBR state (after 
343
370 and 580 days of operation). A homogenised sludge sample of 1.5 mL was transferred into an 
344
Eppendorf tube and centrifuged in a micro-centrifuge (Eppendorf, Hamburg, Germany), to extract 
345
DNA. Around 50 mg of sludge pellet were added to the extraction tubes from the soil FastDNA 
346
spin kit (MP Biomedicals, Irvine, CA, U.S.). DNA extraction was performed following the 
347
protocol established by Albertsen, et al. [7]. The concentration of the extracted DNA was measured 
348
using a Qubit dsDNA assay kit (Thermo Fisher, Waltham, MA, U.S.A). Finally, the DNA samples 
349
were frozen at -25 °C until they were sent for gene amplification. 
350

16S rRNA Gene Amplicon Sequencing and Data Analysis
351
The 16sRNA gene was selected for amplification and paired-end sequence, in an Ilumina 
352
NovaSeq 6000 platform by Novogene (Beijing, China). The primer set chosen to amplify and 
353
sequence the hypervariable region V3-V4 was 341F [50’CCTAYGGGRBGCASCAG–3’] and 
354
806R [5’–GGACTACNNGGGTATCTAAT–3’]. Subsequently, the Sequence Read Archive 
355
(SRA) of the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) was used for reading the raw 
356
data. The gene sequence provided by Novogene was further processed and analysed following the 
357
procedure developed by[8] Amir, et al. [8], Toja Ortega, et al. [9]. 
358
Results and discussion
359
To obtain insight in the micro-organisms specific response to the induced oxygen stress 
360
DNA analysis was performed for the three samples, the AnMBR state (after 150 days of operation), 
361
and two from the MA-AnMBR state (after 370 and 580 days of operation). The two most relatively 


[PDF page 17]
362
abundant taxa for the MA-AnMBR samples were bacteria family Thermofonsia or SBR103 and 
363
archaeal family Methanosaetaceae, while these taxa were the second and fifth most abundant in 
364
the AnMBR samples, respectively. Alpha and beta diversity analyses were conducted to identify 
365
further differences between the two reactor states. The diversity within samples (alpha diversity) 
366
showed that the phylogenetic distance between taxa in each sample was statistically different (p-
367
value of 0.03), whilst the community evenness within samples was insignificant. Even though the 
368
diversity analysis showed a statistical difference between the AnMBR and MA-AnMBR sludge 
369
samples, it cannot be linked to differences in AD performance [10].
370
However, the sequencing of the 16rRNA is not useful to define microorganisms that are 
371
responsible for specific processes [11]. For instance, whilst Methanosaetaceae family relative 
372
abundance increased from 6.2 to 20.7% from the AnMBR to the MA-AnMBR samples (taken after 
373
150 and 370 days respectively), no statistical difference was observed in this group due to the low 
374
relative abundance of Methanosaetaceae in the Archaea kingdom. While most hydrogenotrophic 
375
methanogens are members of the family of Methanobacteriaceae [12], aceticlastic methanogens 
376
belong to the family of Methanosaetaceae [4]. No changes were observed between the sludge 
377
samples from the AnMBR and MA-AnMBR in the relative abundance of both families in the 
378
Archaea kingdom. Methanobacteriaceae represented around 3.5% of the Archaea while the 
379
aceticlastic methanogens were around 95.5%. Since hydrogenotrophic and aceticlastic bacteria’s 
380
