Abstract 
Nanobubble water (NW) has been reported to enhance anaerobic digestion (AD), but its influence on 
the metabolic pathways of microorganisms remains unclear. In this study, the specific methane yields 
of rice straw in the CO2NW and O2NW treatments increased by 6.9% and 18.3%, respectively. The 
electron transport system (ETS) and coenzyme F420 activities were enhanced by the addition of NW. 
Metagenomic analysis showed that the abundances of most enzymes in the acidification were 
significantly increased by both CO2NW and O2NW. Regarding methanogenesis, CO2NW promoted 
the expression of genes encoding enzymes of hydrogenotrophic methanogenesis, while O2NW 
stimulated both the acetoclastic and hydrogenotrophic methanogenesis. With the addition of O2NW, 
the expressions of modules related to the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle and oxidative 
phosphorylation were enhanced, resulting in increased ATP production. This study provided 
fundamental evidence of the metabolic pathways of microorganisms mediated by NW at each stage 
of AD. 
Keywords: Biogas production; Nanobubbles; Rice straw; Micro-oxygen; Microbial community

1. Introduction 
Anaerobic digestion (AD) of crop straw to produce biogas is a representative technique that has 
been widely used in rural bioenergy systems (Sun et al., 2021). However, it faces challenges such as 
long fermentation periods and low specific biogas yields (Fu et al., 2023). Numerous studies related 
to straw pretreatment and AD accelerants have been carried out for higher biogas yield (Kainthola et 
al., 2019). In recent years, interest in nanobubble water (NW) technology has grown as an accelerant 
in the AD process due to its economic and environmentally friendly advantages. 
NW is defined as water containing numerous nanobubbles with diameters smaller than 1000 nm, 
typically ranging from 50 to 200 nm (Chuenchart et al., 2021). Compared with large bubble, it exhibits 
longer residence time in water, the negative surface charge, high gas-liquid mass transfer efficiency, 
and the capability to produce hydroxyl radical (Lyu et al., 2019). X. Wang et al. (2020) reported that 
the highest cumulative methane yield was observed in the reactor with O2NW addition, which was 
30% higher than that of the DW treatment. Song et al. (2023) also reported that the addition of 
CO2NW increased the cumulative methane yield by 17% compared with control group. Although 
adding NW (especially CO2NBW and O2NBW) to AD reactors has been proven to increase biogas 
yields, these studies primarily focus on enhancing AD performance and enzyme activity (Fan et al., 
2020; X. Wang et al., 2020). The explanation of microorganisms remains focused on the microbial 
community characteristics interpreted through 16S rRNA high-throughput sequencing. However, 16S 
rRNA sequencing can only provide information about the relative abundance of microbial community 
composition, which limits the understanding of specific metabolic pathways during AD (Jünemann 
et al., 2017). Metagenomic analysis, a next-generation sequencing technology, has been widely 
utilized to reveal changes in key microbial genes encoding enzymes at different stages of the AD 
process (hydrolysis, acidification, and methanogenesis), while elucidating the detailed mechanisms 
of the metabolic interactions of different microbial populations (Kim et al., 2022). To the best of 
authors' knowledge, there have been no studies on the effects of NW addition on metabolic pathways 
using metagenomics analysis. 
Therefore, it is necessary to elucidate the mechanisms underlying the effects of NW on 
microorganisms and metabolic pathways by combining 16S rRNA sequencing and metagenomics. 
First, substrate degradation and methane production from rice straw with the addition of CO2NW and 
O2NW were investigated. Then, the potential functional mechanisms of the effects of NW addition 
were elucidated by examining related enzyme activities and microbial community structure. Finally, 
the effects of NW addition on the metabolic pathways of lignocellulosic AD process were determined 
using metagenomic analysis. This study aimed to fill the knowledge gap regarding NW-promoted 
metabolism and identify the influence of NW on key enzymes at different stages of the AD process 
(hydrolysis, acidification, and methanogenesis).

2.2 Batch anaerobic digestion experiment 
This study was conducted to investigate the influence of NW on biogas production from rice 
straw. The experiments were performed in 250-mL serum bottles, which were placed in a water bath 
at 37 ± 2 ℃ to produce biogas. The rice straw and inoculum were loaded at a ratio of 1:1.5, based on 
volatile solids (VS). The total solids (TS) content of all bottles was adjusted to 5% by adding DW, 
CO2NW, or O2NW, labeled as DW, CO2NW, or O2NW treatment, respectively. Each treatment was 
tested in triplicate. Three bottles containing only the inoculum were labeled as blank. The serum 
bottles were purged with N2 to remove air in the bottle before sealing. The bottles were manually 
shaken daily to ensure uniform mixing. The biogas volumes were periodically measured using 
syringes and normalized to standard temperature and pressure (273.1 K and 1 atm) condition. The 
batch AD experiment was performed for 45 days until biogas production ceased.

3. Results and discussion 
3.1 Effect of nanobubble water on methane production 
The cumulative and daily methane yields from the DW, CO2NW, and O2NW treatments are 
shown in Fig.1. After 45 days of AD, the specific methane yields for the CO2NW and O2NW 
treatments were 257.4 ± 2.5 and 284.8 ± 2.0 NmL/gVS, respectively, which were 6.9% and 18.3% 
higher than that of the DW treatment (p < 0.05). Additionally, the daily methane yields for the DW, 
CO2NW, and O2NW treatments peaked on the 6th and 7th days, reaching 18.9, 22.0, and 27.6 
NmL/gVS/d, respectively. The significant increase of the peak of daily methane yield in CO2NW and 
O2NW, which also demonstrated that adding NW in the AD process could benefit to accelerate biogas 
production. Previous studies have reported similar findings, in which a two-stage food waste AD 
system based on NW showed a higher peak daily yield compared with the DW treatment (Hou et al., 
2021). In addition, the highest yield being obtained under the O2NW treatment could be attributed to 
the increased facultative bacteria activity due to the presence of a small amount of exogenous oxygen, 
which facilitated more efficient organic material decomposition and increased biogas production 
(Nguyen et al., 2019). 
The kinetic parameters are presented in Table 1. All treatments achieved R2 > 0.99, indicating 
good fitting. The lag phases (λ) for the DW, CO2NW, and O2NW treatments were 3.6, 2.7, and 3.1, 
respectively, indicating that NW could decrease the fermentation time and accelerate biogas 
production. The O2NW treatment exhibited the highest maximum methane potential yield (Pm) and 
maximum methane yield rate (Rm) values at 263.5 ± 5.5 mL/gVS and 20.1 ± 0.5 mL/gVS/d, 
respectively. This result demonstrated a significant increase compared with the DW and CO2NW 
treatments, which was consistent with the experimental results.

producing highly reactive hydroxyl radicals. The strong oxidizing ability of these radicals might have 
caused the higher lignocellulose degradation rate in the O2NW treatment.

3.2.2 VFAs production 
VFAs were main intermediate metabolites utilized by methanogenic archaea in the AD process. 
The VFAs concentrations for each treatment are shown in Fig. 2b. The VFAs concentrations in all 
treatments rapidly increased and peaked within the first 6 days. On the 6th day, the total VFAs 
concentration in the DW and CO2NW treatments reached 421.0 ± 58.5 mg/L and 545.1 ± 5.7 mg/L, 
respectively. And the O2NW treatment obtained the highest total VFAs concentration at 898.5 ± 55.4 
mg/L, which was 112.7% and 64.3% higher than that of the DW and CO2NW treatments, respectively. 
This result indicated that adding O2NW could promote VFAs production and the organic matter 
degradation. The VFAs concentration exhibited an initial increase, followed by a decrease, eventually 
reaching a dynamic balance between production and consumption until the end of AD. This trend was 
consistent with the daily methane production (as discussed in Section 3.1). 
Among the VFAs compositions, acetate was the main component in the early stages, whereas 
propionate became predominant after 6 days. Generally, acetate could be directly utilized by 
methanogens, while propionate needed to be converted into acetate before being further utilized. The 
Gibbs free energy (ΔG) of the propionate degradation process was positive, indicating 
thermodynamic disadvantage, which resulted in propionate accumulation during the initial stages 
(Wu et al., 2022). Additionally, the O2NW treatment exhibited higher propionate concentrations 
compared with DW and CO2NW treatments. Some researchers have reported that higher oxidation-
reduction potential (ORP) levels in a micro-oxygen environment might promote propionic acid 
production (Cohen et al., 1984; Zhuge et al., 2015). However, its concentration did not reach the 
threshold level. The previous study reported that propionic acid concentration above 900 mg/L 
adversely affected the growth of methanogens (Wang et al., 2009). Furthermore, the propionate 
concentration fell below the detection limit after 30 days of fermentation. This indicated that, despite 
the dominance of propionic acid, it did not reach a level sufficient to hinder biogas production or 
cause acidification.

the digestate, which indirectly indicated the microbial metabolic activity (Lizama et al., 2019; Tian

et al., 2017). The changes in ETS of each treatment are shown in Fig. 3a. The highest ETS activity 
was exhibited in O2NW treatment on the 6th day, increasing by 41.6% and 14.3% compared with the 
DW and CO2NW treatments, respectively. This indicated that O2NW could promote microbial 
activity to accelerate substrate hydrolysis and acidification. Oxygen in O2NW could function as an 
electron acceptor in the oxidation of NADH, releasing significant energy to drive ATP synthesis and 
the intermediates transfer (X. Wang et al., 2020). On the other hand, negatively charged nanobubbles 
had a stronger attraction to cations, which might accelerate the mass transfer of intermediates such as 
NAD+ (Kyzas et al., 2019). Therefore, the increase in ETS activity might be the reason why the O2NW 
treatment achieved the highest methane production. Besides, the ETS activity showed an increasing 
trend during the early stages of AD, followed by a gradual decreased over time. Pearson correlation 
analysis also showed a positive correlation between ETS activity and VFA (See supplementary 
materials). 
Coenzyme F420 played an important role in the methanogenic activity (Zhao et al., 2018). At the 
end of AD process, the coenzyme F420 activity were 6.9 ± 0.5, 8.6 ± 0.3, and 9.7 ± 0.8 μmol/L in the 
DW CO2NW and O2NW treatments, respectively (Fig. 3b). The significantly highest activity of 
coenzyme F420 obtained in O2NW treatment might be the reason for the high specific methane 
production. These results agreed with those of a previous study that a 34% increase in coenzyme F420
activity by adding AirNW to food waste AD compared with the DW treatment (Hou et al., 2021). 
Additionally, Nguyen et al. (2019) reported that O2 had the potential to oxidize VFAs into CO2, 
consequently increasing the production of CH4. Coenzyme F420 was an essential enzyme for 
hydrogenotrophic methanogenesis (M. Wang et al., 2023). Thus, O2NW potentially stimulated the 
growth of hydrogenotrophic methanogens by oxidizing VFAs into CO2 and promoting the secretion 
of coenzyme F420.

3.3 Effect of nanobubble water on microbial community 
The microbial community was analyzed based on 16S rRNA sequencing to elucidate the impact 
of NW during the AD process. The coverage of all samples exceeded 0.99, suggesting that the results 
accurately represented the actual state of the microbial community (See supplementary materials). 
Compared with the DW treatment, the CO2NW and O2NW treatments showed higher Shannon and 
lower Simpson values of bacteria. It indicated that CO2NW and O2NW were increase in species 
diversity of acid-producing bacteria. However, the indices of archaea showed the opposite trend, 
which indicated NW resulted in lower archaeal diversity due to their heightened sensitivity to the 
environment. Species richness was often measured using the ACE and Chao 1 indices (Zhu et al., 
2022), and the results showed that NW did not significantly affect the richness of bacteria and archaea. 
The 
Firmicutes, 
Caldatribacteriota, 
Synergistota, 
Bacteroidota, 
Chloroflexi, 
and 
Actinobacteriota were the predominant bacterial phyla in each treatment (Fig. 4a). These bacteria 
were typically associated with cellulose degradation and organic acid production. The CO2NW and 
O2NW treatments significantly decreased the relative abundance of Firmicutes compared with the 
DW treatment, from 78.3% to 46.3% and 62.5%, respectively. However, in the CO2NW and O2NW 
treatments, Caldatribacteriota, Synergistota, Bacteroidota, Chloroflexi, and Actinobacteriota
exhibited higher relative abundance compared with the DW treatment.

methanogenesis. Therefore, the enrichment of Syntrophomonas and Petrimonas in the O2NW 
treatment was beneficial for VFAs oxidation by promoting DIET-based syntrophic metabolism. 
However, owing to the relatively low total abundance of electroactive bacteria, such as 
Syntrophomonas and Petrimonas, which were found to be 2.8%, 3.7%, and 4.6% in the DW, CO2NW, 
and O2NW treatments, respectively. Further validation of DIET involving electroactive bacteria and 
methanogens is still required in future research. 
Among the archaeal, at the genus level, Methanosarcina and Methanobacterium were 
predominant in all treatments (Fig. 4c). The relative abundances of Methanosarcina in the CO2NW 
and O2NW treatments were 84.7% and 82.0%, respectively, representing a significant increase 
compared with the DW treatment (59.2%). However, the relative abundances of Methanobacterium
decreased from 40.7% (in the DW treatment) to 14.5% and 17.5%, respectively. Some studies have 
reported that Methanosarcina is resistant to micro-oxygen conditions (Angel et al., 2012) and high 
ammonia nitrogen environments (De Vrieze et al., 2012). In contrast, other archaea were typically 
damaged by these adverse environments, allowing Methanosarcina to occupy a larger ecological 
niche (Ma et al., 2015). The behavior may explain the observed enrichment of Methanosarcina in the 
NW treatment. Methanobacterium, an obligate hydrogenotrophic methanogen (Wu et al., 2021), was 
another important archaeon identified in this study. Although the relative abundance of 
Methanobacterium significantly decreased in the NW treatments, Methanosarcina, a typical 
mixotrophic methanogen, exhibited an increasing trend. Methanosarcina has the capability to 
produce methane through acetoclastic, hydrogenotrophic, and methylotrophic pathways (Ma et al., 
2015). 
In this study, the addition of CO2NW might have provided more CO2 for the fermentation system 
to participate in hydrogenotrophic methanogenesis. Moreover, CO2NW might alter the partial 
pressure of CO2 and equilibrium of the carbonate system in AD reactors (Antonio Cerrón-Calle et al., 
2022). Thermodynamically, the increased partial pressure of CO2 favors hydrogenotrophic 
methanogenesis (HM) over acetoclastic methanogenesis (AM). However, in the CO2NW treatment, 
the relative abundance of hydrogenotrophic methanogens did not observably increase, whereas that

3.4 Metabolic pathway analysis 
To further elucidate the mechanism by which NW promoted AD process, metagenomic analysis 
was performed. The abundance of AD-related pathways in both the CO2NW and O2NW treatment 
was significantly higher than that of the DW treatment (See supplementary materials). The addition 
of NW promoted substrate metabolism (carbohydrates and amino acids) and energy metabolism 
(oxidative phosphorylation and methane metabolism), which might have been the reason for the 
higher methane production. We also observed that O2NW increased the abundance of ABC transports 
pathway. This indicates that O2NW could accelerate the transmembrane transport of organic material, 
which might stimulate the growth of acidogenic and methanogenic microorganisms.

Fig. 5 illustrates the key metabolism pathways and functional enzymes from the KEGG database 
during the hydrolysis, acidogenesis, and methanogenesis stages. Considering that crop straw mainly 
consisted of cellulose and hemicellulose, its hydrolysis products mainly included hexoses (like 
glucose) and pentoses (like xylose) (Wu et al., 2021). The cellulase (EC: 3.2.1.4), glucosidase (EC: 
3.2.1.21), xylanase (EC: 3.2.1.8), and xylosidase (EC: 3.2.1.37) were key enzymes related to cellulose 
and hemicellulose hydrolysis. For the acidogenesis stage, glucose, the cellulose hydrolysis product, 
was converted into glucose-6P by hexokinase (EC: 2.7.1.1) and glucokinase (EC: 2.7.1.2), and then 
decomposed into pyruvate through glycolysis (Embden-Meyerhof pathway, EMP) or the pentose 
phosphate pathway (PPP). Hemicellulose, another major component of crop straw, was hydrolyzed 
into xylose and glucose. Xylose was converted into xylulose-5P through the xylose metabolism and 
then entered the PPP to form pyruvate. Pyruvate was an important intermediate metabolite in VFAs 
and methane production (Zhu et al., 2022) and was converted into acetyl-CoA by oxidative 
decarboxylation. Subsequently, acetyl-CoA could be further converted into acetic acid via either a 
two-step reaction catalyzed by phosphate acetyltransferase (EC: 2.3.1.8) and acetate kinase (EC:

addition of CO2NW and O2NW significantly increased the abundance of most enzymes in the 
acidification stage, rather than the hydrolysis enzymes. This suggests that the addition of NW was 
more effective in promoting acidification (intracellular metabolism) compared with hydrolysis

environment induced by the NW, resulting in differences the expression of genes encoding hydrolytic 
and acidifying enzyme. The previous studies only utilizing 16S rRNA sequencing, which also

In the traditional AD process (without oxygen), glucose could only be degraded via the EMP to 
produce small amounts of NADH and ATP by substrate-level phosphorylation. However, the addition 
of O2NW alters the strictly anaerobic environment, which could create micro-oxygen environment 
(X. Wang et al., 2020). During the substrate metabolism, exogenous O2 was the preferred electron 
acceptor because of its thermodynamics (Nguyen and Khanal, 2018). Thus, the energy metabolism 
exhibited differences compared with that in the strictly anaerobic environment (DW treatment); 
particularly significant increases in the expression of oxidative phosphorylation module in the O2NW 
treatment were observed. In addition, the expression of most functional enzymes involved in the 
tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle was significantly upregulated. As the TCA cycle was activated under 
the microaerobic conditions, partial acetyl-CoA molecules could be completely oxidized to CO2, 
generating NADH in the process (Xiao et al., 2024). The produced NADH was then completely 
oxidized to synthesize a large amount of ATP by oxidative phosphorylation system (Yang et 
al., 2024). The upregulation of key enzymes involved in oxidative phosphorylation, such as NADH 
dehydrogenase (EC: 7.1.1.2), succinate dehydrogenase (EC: 1.3.5.1), cytochrome c oxidase (EC: 
7.1.1.9), and ATP synthase (EC: 7.1.2.2), was also a manifestation of enhanced oxidative 
phosphorylation by O2NW (Fig 5). The increased abundance of NADH dehydrogenase helps to 
prevent the accumulation of NADH/NAD+, which promotes substrate degradation and organic acid 
formation. The expression of cytochrome c oxidase also reflected a similar upregulation trend in the 
O2NW environment. Those results suggested that O2NW (micro-oxygen environment) could promote 
ATP synthesis and stimulates higher energy metabolism, which was beneficial for providing energy 
for substrate degradation and cellular processes such as metabolism, growth, and signal transduction. 
In general, the predominantly expression of bacteria is F-type ATPase (M00157), whereas 
archaea employ V/A-type ATPase (M00159) to catalyze the conversion of ADP to ATP (Zhu et al., 
2022). The abundance of ATPase in the O2NW treatment was higher than that in both the DW and 
CO2NW treatments, particularly a significant increase in F-type ATPase was obtained (See 
supplementary materials). This suggests that the enrichment of F-type ATPase associated with the 
acidification stage by adding O2NW was an important factor in improving biogas production.

analyzed, as shown in Fig.5, based on map00680. In the AM pathway, acetate—CoA ligase (EC:

route involves the conversion of acetate to acetyl-CoA by the acetate kinase (EC: 2.7.2.1) and 
phosphate acetyltransferase (EC: 2.3.1.8). This conversion is strongly related to the higher abundance

O2NW additions. The key enzymes of the shared nodes of acetoclastic and hydrogenotrophic

3.5 Implications and future perspective of nanobubble water mediated anaerobic digestion 
process 
This study revealed the succession of microbial communities and metabolic pathways with the 
addition of NW in the AD process, which may provide fundamental evidence for the mediation of 
nanobubbles at each stage of AD. The gene expressions of glycolysis, pyruvate metabolism, and fatty 
acid metabolism were obviously upregulated due to the exist of CO2NW or O2NW. Meanwhile, the 
enhancement of the oxidative phosphorylation pathway and the TCA cycle in the O2NW treatment 
could provide more energy for substrate conversion, mass transfer, and microbial metabolism activity. 
Therefore, nanobubble technology combined with AD can serve as a low-cost and environmentally 
friendly biogas-upgrading strategy. 
However, there are still some problems in this work that need further investigation. For instance, 
O2NW increases the ORP of the AD system and promotes propionic acid formation, potentially 
resulting in acidification or even failure of continuously operating reactors. Secondly, although the 
addition of NW increased the specific methane yield, it downregulated the abundance of extracellular 
enzymes involved in the hydrolysis process. Further research is needed to explore the interactions 
between nanobubbles and cells, and to reveal how nanobubbles mediate extracellular and intracellular 
metabolic mechanism. Finally, the O2NW treatment increased the abundance of electroactive bacteria 
(Syntrophomonas and Petrimonas) in this study, potentially enhancing DIET for increased biogas 
production. However, due to only a microbial community test was performed and other evidence 
(such as electron transfer capacity) to support the DIET enhancement is lacking, further validation is 
required.

4. Conclusion 
This study revealed mechanism by which CO2-nanobubble water (CO2NW) and O2NW 
mediated the anaerobic digestion process. Metagenomic analysis revealed that CO2NW specifically 
enhanced the expression of enzymes for the hydrogenotrophic methanogenesis pathway, whereas 
O2NW stimulated both the acetoclastic and hydrogenotrophic methanogenesis. The micro-oxygen 
environment created by O2NW upregulated the energy metabolism module, including tricarboxylic 
acid cycle and oxidative phosphorylation, thereby promoting ATP production. Furthermore, O2NW 
increased substrate degradation rate and enhanced the activities of electron transport system and 
coenzyme F420. The above results explained that the cumulative methane yields were increased by 
6.9% and 18.3% for CO2NBW and O2NBW, respectively.
