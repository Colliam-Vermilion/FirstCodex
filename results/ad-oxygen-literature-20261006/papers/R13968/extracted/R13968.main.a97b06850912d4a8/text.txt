5.1 Conclusions


[{"type": "pdf_block", "pdf_page": 1, "pdf_page_label": "", "block": 3, "bbox_points": [106.70100402832031, 220.7889862060547, 517.9303588867188, 344.69085693359375]}]
The effects of microaeration on separate hydrogen and methane production 
from ethanol wastewater, was investigated in the two-stage UASB system consisting 
of 4 and 24 litter hydrogen and methane working volume, respectively. The system 
was operated at a mesophillic temperature and a recycle ratio of 1:1, with controlling 
pH of 5.5 in hydrogen production tank and without pH control in methane production 
tank.


[{"type": "pdf_block", "pdf_page": 1, "pdf_page_label": "", "block": 4, "bbox_points": [106.23095703125, 346.0888366699219, 518.3169555664062, 617.585693359375]}]
The scope of research was separated into two parts. The first part was to study 
the optimum COD loading rate for separate hydrogen and methane production in two- 
stage UASB system. The result showed the optimal COD loading rate was obtained at 
6.0 kg/m3 d based on methane working volume (or 36.0 kg/m3 d based on hydrogen 
working volume), which is the most suitable condition for microbial growth. At an 
optimal COD loading rate, the hydrogen composition (41 %), gas production rate (4.5 
1/d), COD removal (36.0 %), hydrogen yield (33.5 ml I-h/g COD removed or 12.0 ml 
H2/g COD applied), SHPR (460.8 ml H2/LR d and 79.3 ml H2/g MLVSS d) and total 
VFA (9,400 mg/1 as acetic acid) were maximized. At the same optimal COD loading 
rate, the performance of methane production reached methane composition (69.6 %), 
gas production rate (27.5 1/d), COD removal (55.0 %), methane yield (165.5 ml CH4/g 
COD removed or 62.5 ml CHVg COD applied), SMPR (797.5 ml CH4/LR d and 98.0 
ml CH4/g MLVSS d) and total VFA (226.9 mg/1 as acetic acid)

5.2 Recommendations


[{"type": "pdf_block", "pdf_page": 2, "pdf_page_label": "", "block": 4, "bbox_points": [106.25, 222.93907165527344, 517.0715942382812, 326.29315185546875]}]
It is interesting to study effects of microaeration on hydrogen production 
using ethanol wastewater under the dark fermentation by two-stage UASB system. In 
addition, microaeration technique may be applied to other systems, such as anaerobic 
sludge blanket reactor (ASBR) or continuous stirred tank reactor (CSTR), or photo 
fermentation including the various types of wastewater.
